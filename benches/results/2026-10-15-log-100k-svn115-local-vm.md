# Benchmark: log over 100,000 revisions, OpenSVN engine vs svn CLI

## Run 3 (3 repetitions, CPU time recorded)

- svn CLI 1.15.0; server: Apache/2.4.69 (Unix) SVN/1.15.0, HTTP on 127.0.0.1
- repository: 100000 revisions, each but r1 changing one of 100 files
- machine: Intel(R) Xeon(R) Processor, 4 CPUs, 15.7 GiB RAM, Linux 6.18.44-fc-v114
- filesystem of the repository: ext4 on /dev/vda (rw,relatime,discard,no_prefetch_block_bitmaps,resv_strict,resuid=65534,resgid=65534); NOT journaled (no jbd2 journal for this device)
- build: release
- repetitions: 3 (median shown, min in brackets; every repetition below)

| operation | svn CLI | engine | engine / svn |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| log, all 100000 revisions (`-q`) | 6.28 s (1.84) | 1.65 s (1.62) | 0.26 |
| log, all 100000 revisions with changed paths (`-v`) | 3.51 s (2.23) | 2.70 s (2.52) | 0.77 |
| log, newest 100 (`-q -l 100`) | 0.06 s (0.02) | 0.02 s (0.02) | 0.31 |
| log of one file (1000 revisions, `-q`) | 0.06 s (0.04) | 0.03 s (0.02) | 0.43 |

## Notes

- The wall time of a full log is mostly the server's (mod_dav_svn walking 100,000 revisions)
  and varies from 1.6 to 8.5 s for the same request on this VM. Over the three runs the median
  ratio engine / svn is 2.04, 1.05, 0.26 for `-q` and 2.06, 1.02, 0.77 for `-v`: the order of the
  clients against the server's caches and the VM decide it, not the client.
- The client's own work is 3 to 4 times smaller in the engine: 0.5 s of CPU for 100,000 entries
  without changed paths and 0.75 s with them, against 1.8 s and 2.2 s for svn (which also formats
  and prints them; the engine only counts). The engine is not the bottleneck of a log.
- Run 1 had two engine repetitions of about 4.9 s next to 1.4 s on the same request. They did not
  come back in the 8 repetitions of runs 2 and 3; the engine's CPU time stayed at 0.5 s, so the
  extra time was spent waiting on the server or the VM, not in the engine.
- `-l 100` and the log of one file (1,000 entries) take 10 to 60 ms for both clients.
